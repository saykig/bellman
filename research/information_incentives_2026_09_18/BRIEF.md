# Information, incentives, and strategic disclosure — research brief

18 September 2026. New research phase, superseding the earlier revision-focused
question as the active task. Historical work and supplied Downloads artifacts
remain unchanged. No branches; preserve the existing uncommitted clarification.

Question: when do partial information structures and incentive conditions compose
into an exact cooperation guarantee? Which compatibility data must survive, and
can the supporting information arrangement survive voluntary verifiable disclosure?

Required work: (1) full/shared-budget versus statewise-envelope versus richer
intermediate experiment, with an active operational incentive; (2) analytical
composition/sharpness result, not only a support-function reformulation; (3) a
solved fixed-enforcement disclosure model, followed by its joint frontier; (4)
closest-source comparison, counterexamples, reproducible evidence and final note.

Inputs: the supplied binary frontier note (§12 active operations), supplied
access-envelope note, and independent 18 September audit. Exact input identities
are in sources/INPUT_MANIFEST.json. The audit's 0.08042888249 versus 0.32485021344
penalty comparison at κ=.2, γ=.8 is a regression, not a new result of this phase.
The prior SLSQP disagreements are retained as a reason to check primal witnesses
and dual bounds, not to trust optimizer status alone.

Provisional theorem path: retain a minimal information-cost function for each
message likelihood, sharing one budget across payoff states. Use tree marginal
compatibility to compute these functions through augmented local tables when a
Markov reference and finite transcript flags apply. Test the resulting exact
representation against intervals. Separately test a verifiable state certificate
held by a third party who benefits from one player's state-matching policy.

Distinctions: probability compatibility is existence of a joint law; the information
budget restricts such laws; obedience is an incentive inequality under that law;
enforcement changes the payoff of an observable policy deviation. Analyst ambiguity
is over laws, whereas players know the actual law but not realized states. A gate
changes a signal mechanism; changing its penalty changes incentives. Neither
certifies the enforcer's credibility, participation, welfare, or empirical truth.

No claim of historical novelty or full Lean verification is a completion shortcut.
