# Rejected claims and scope decisions

1. **Only posterior support and existence of an equilibrium can explain jumps.**
   Rejected. Two-law budget admission jumps with full type support and unchanged
   receiver continuations. Admissible-law lower continuity is also needed.
2. **Cost variation refutes the information-only conjecture.** Rejected as an
   interpretation after the user's clarification. The cost example is retained
   for the full-parameter layer; Theorem I fixes those costs.
3. **Every support change causes a jump.** Rejected. Controlled enforcement may
   already equal H; the exact masking condition is κ≥κC.
4. **A receiver equilibrium exists, so a deterrent continuation persists.**
   Rejected. In the δ example some best response always exists, but B disappears.
5. **Choose the cheapest continuation independently for each sender type.**
   Rejected. One public continuation must simultaneously deter all eligible types.
6. **The supplied-gate jump establishes discontinuous optimal design.** Rejected.
   The designer's feasible noisy gate removes it in this family.
7. **Claim a new general boundary theorem.** Rejected. Berge-type reasoning and
   feasible-path-transfer continuity already provide the general foundation.
   The finite-phase criterion is elementary; novelty of the specialization is open.
8. **Reuse the algebraic Lean threshold after an eligible type vanishes.**
   Rejected. It permits unsupported posterior weights; the game interpretation now
   has an explicit full-support wrapper. Singleton cases require their own proof.
9. **Expand to networks, multiple senders or engineering integration.** Deferred.
   None is needed to settle the present mechanism. Public information, known law,
   weak pooling-PBE existence and favorable ties remain declared restrictions.
10. **Promote numerical optimizer agreement to formal verification.** Rejected.
    The unsuccessful solver status is preserved. Theoretical proofs, finite exact
    witnesses, numerical tests and the bounded Lean check are separate evidence.
